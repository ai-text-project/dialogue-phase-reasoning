# Rationale for External Route Governance

## Background

The research program originally operated under a largely single-purpose structure.

Primary activity focused on:

- observation
- documentation
- classification
- research-space development

---

## Emergence of External Routes

As the research program matured, additional activities emerged.

Examples included:

- Harvest Strategy
- Career Exploration
- Research Community Exploration

These activities introduced new perspectives concerning:

- value
- employability
- communication
- social connection

---

## Emerging Concern

A question emerged:

Can multiple external-facing initiatives coexist without altering the internal direction of the research program?

The concern was not exploration itself.

The concern was unintended reverse influence.

Examples included:

- modifying research for employability
- modifying research for popularity
- modifying research for marketability
- modifying research for community growth

---

## Observation

External initiatives appeared useful and potentially valuable.

However, their objectives differed from those of the core research program.

This suggested the need for explicit governance.

---

## Interpretation

A governance structure was proposed in which:

Research Program

↓

Research Assets

↓

External Routes

The relationship is outward-facing.

External routes may observe and support the research program.

They do not govern it.

---

## Result

Research Program Protection Policy v1 was established.

Its purpose is to allow:

- Employment Route
- Harvest Route
- Community Route

to develop independently while preserving the integrity of the research program.
