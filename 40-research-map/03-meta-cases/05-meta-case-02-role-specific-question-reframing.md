# Role-Specific Question Reframing

---

## Status

Meta Observation

Provisional Meta Case

Version: v0.1

---

## Purpose

This document records a provisional meta observation regarding repeated changes in the primary inquiry during role-specific dialogues.

The purpose is not to introduce a new theoretical framework.

Instead, it documents how different operational roles may progressively reorganize the questions they ask while leaving the underlying research program unchanged.

---

## Core Observation

Repeated dialogue may reveal that the primary object of development is not the production of new artifacts.

Instead, the inquiry itself gradually changes.

Typical progression:

Question A

↓

Question B

↓

Question C

↓

Question D

Each reformulation changes the operational perspective while preserving the underlying research program.

---

## Typical Characteristics

Role-specific question reframing often includes:

* progressive refinement of the central question
* shifts in operational perspective
* reinterpretation of existing assets
* emergence of new evaluation criteria
* changes in the role's own viewpoint

The research program itself may remain unchanged.

The observed evolution occurs primarily within the operational framing.

---

---

## Why This Is a Meta Observation

The primary observation object is not:

* a theory
* a repository
* a document
* an operational asset

Instead, the observation concerns how the role itself progressively changes its framing of the problem.

The evolution occurs at the level of observation strategy.

---

## Potential Future Comparisons

Future role-specific dialogues may reveal similar patterns, for example:

If comparable sequences are repeatedly observed, they may provide evidence for broader patterns of role-dependent inquiry evolution.

## Current Interpretation

This document should be regarded as a provisional Meta Observation.

Current evidence is based primarily on one operational role.

Additional observations from other roles will be required before considering this a broader research pattern.
