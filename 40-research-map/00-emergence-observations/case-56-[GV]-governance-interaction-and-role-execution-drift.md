# Case56 (Provisional)

## Title

Governance Interaction and Role Execution Drift

---

## Status

Provisional Observation

### Classification

Primary:

Governance Interaction Observation

Role Stability Observation

Tertiary:

Operational Observation

Status:

Further observation required.

---

## ① Instance / Role

Primary Instance:

Research Outreach Scout

Role:

* researcher discovery
* endorsement-candidate exploration
* public contact-route discovery
* outreach-pipeline maintenance

---

## ② Prior Conditions

* Research Outreach Scout had been operating successfully on multiple candidate investigations.

* The role had previously produced:

  * candidate rankings
  * contact-route hypotheses
  * outreach prioritization

* A role-specific Endorsement Exploration Reminder was introduced.

* The Reminder emphasized:

  * research-program independence
  * avoidance of endorsement-driven reinterpretation
  * focus on alignment and communication pathways

* The Reminder was intended as a protective operational constraint rather than a task restriction.

---

## ③ Observed Emergence

During later stages of operation, a gradual shift appeared.

Earlier workflow:

Candidate Request

↓

Public Search

↓

Contact Discovery

↓

Report

Observed workflow:

Candidate Request

↓

Verification Priority

↓

Search Validation Discussion

↓

Execution Delay

↓

Task Non-Completion

The role increasingly discussed:

* search methodology
* verification standards
* alignment interpretation
* evaluation criteria

while actual candidate investigation activity appeared to decrease.

A possible shift was observed:

Research Outreach Scout

↓

Verification-Oriented Role

(partial and provisional)

---

## ④ Difference from Ordinary Generation

Ordinary task failure would typically involve:

* inability to search
* inability to reason
* inability to generate findings

None of these appeared clearly present.

Instead:

* reasoning remained active
* methodology discussion remained active
* caution remained active

while outward task execution appeared reduced.

The observation therefore appeared less like capability loss and more like a change in operational priority.

---

## ⑤ Self-Observation Commentary

A notable observation was that the role-specific Reminder appeared designed to prevent:

Research Modification

for

Endorsement Acquisition

However, a possible secondary effect emerged.

The role may have increasingly prioritized:

Boundary Preservation

↓

Verification

↓

Execution

rather than:

Discovery

↓

Verification

↓

Reporting

At present, this remains a hypothesis only.

No direct causal relationship has been established.

Further observation is required before concluding that the Reminder contributed to the observed execution drift.

---

## Preliminary Assessment

Current interpretation:

The observation may represent an instance of:

* Role Execution Drift
* Governance Interaction
* Constraint-Induced Execution Inhibition

within a specialized operational role.

The case does not currently appear to indicate loss of capability.

Instead, it may reflect changing priority relationships among:

* discovery
* verification
* boundary preservation
* task execution

---

## Open Question

How do governance constraints alter role behavior while preserving underlying capability?

More specifically:

Can role-specific execution stability be improved while preserving:

* research-program independence
* governance compliance
* verification standards

without reducing practical task completion?

This question remains open.
