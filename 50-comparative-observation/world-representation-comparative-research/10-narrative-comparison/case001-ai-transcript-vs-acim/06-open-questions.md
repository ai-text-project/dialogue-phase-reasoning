# Open Questions

## Purpose

This chapter summarizes the principal questions that emerged during the comparative analysis.

These questions are not regarded as deficiencies of the present case.

Instead, they represent observations that require further comparative investigation.

Accordingly, the purpose of this chapter is to identify future research directions rather than to resolve them.

---

# Open Question 1

## What is "World"?

Throughout the comparative analysis, both frameworks repeatedly referred to the concept of *World*.

However, the structural role assigned to this concept differed considerably.

The AI transcript implicitly assumed an external environment that supports observation and interaction.

ACIM emphasized the experienced world as inseparable from perception.

This raises a fundamental question.

Does the term *World* refer to the same conceptual object across different frameworks, or does each worldview construct its own notion of world?

---

# Open Question 2

## What is Representation?

Both frameworks require an internal representation.

However, the function of that representation differs.

Within the AI transcript, representation supports adaptive interaction with the environment.

Within ACIM, representation reflects the current condition of perception and interpretation.

This raises a broader question.

Is there a more general concept of representation that encompasses both operational modeling and perceptual organization?

---

# Open Question 3

## Is Prediction Always Explicit?

Prediction occupies a central position within the AI transcript.

ACIM does not explicitly describe prediction as a computational process.

Nevertheless, present perception appears to influence future experience.

This raises the question of whether prediction can exist implicitly through representational transformation rather than explicit forecasting.

---

# Open Question 4

## What Constitutes Action?

The AI transcript emphasizes action directed toward the external environment.

ACIM emphasizes internal choice and correction of perception.

This raises an important structural question.

Can internal transformation itself be regarded as a form of action?

If so, how should action be defined across different world representations?

---

# Open Question 5

## Can Intelligence Be Defined Independently of World Representation?

The comparative analysis suggests that each framework develops its concept of intelligence from its underlying representation of the world.

This observation raises a broader research question.

Is there a universal concept of intelligence, or is every definition of intelligence inseparable from a particular worldview?

---

# Open Question 6

## Can World Representations Be Compared Systematically?

The present case demonstrates that two independently reconstructed world representations can be compared through a common structural perspective.

However, it remains unclear whether this approach can be generalized.

Future case studies will be required to determine whether the same comparative framework can be applied to additional theories, philosophies, and cognitive systems.

---

# Research Outlook

The questions presented above do not conclude the research.

Instead, they define the next stage of investigation.

Each question has the potential to become an independent comparative case.

Accordingly, the present case should be regarded as the beginning of an expanding research program rather than a completed theoretical framework.
