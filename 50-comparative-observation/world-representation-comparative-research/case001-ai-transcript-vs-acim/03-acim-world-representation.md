# ACIM World Representation

## Purpose

This chapter reconstructs the internal world representation presented in *A Course in Miracles (ACIM).*

The objective is not to compare ACIM with artificial intelligence, cognitive science, or any other framework.

Instead, ACIM is treated as an independent worldview whose internal structure is reconstructed as faithfully as possible.

The comparative analysis will be conducted only after both world representations have been reconstructed independently.

---

# Observation Overview

Within ACIM, the central concern is not the external world itself, but the relationship between perception, mind, and reality.

The Course repeatedly distinguishes between what appears to be experienced and what is ultimately real.

Consequently, the focus of ACIM is not primarily the prediction or control of external events, but the correction of perception.

From this perspective, the world is understood primarily through the condition of the perceiving mind rather than through objective observation alone.

---

# Fundamental Components

The reconstructed world representation consists of the following core components.

## World

Within ACIM, the world is experienced through perception.

The Course distinguishes between the world that appears to perception and ultimate Reality.

Accordingly, the experienced world is not treated as self-sufficient, but as dependent upon the condition of the mind.

---

## Perception

Perception functions as the immediate experience through which the world is encountered.

However, perception is not regarded as inherently reliable.

Instead, it reflects the condition of the perceiver and therefore may either conceal or reveal truth.

---

## Representation

The internal representation of the world is formed within the mind.

Rather than serving as a neutral model of external reality, this representation reflects interpretation, belief, and identification.

Transformation therefore occurs primarily through changes within the mind rather than through modification of the external world.

---

## Choice

ACIM presents choice as a fundamental process within the mind.

Rather than emphasizing physical action, the Course emphasizes the continual choice between different systems of interpretation.

This choice determines how the world is perceived.

---

## Correction

Correction refers to the transformation of perception.

Instead of predicting future states of the world, ACIM emphasizes the correction of mistaken perception through a change in internal interpretation.

The central movement is therefore from misperception toward corrected perception.

---

## Intelligence

ACIM does not explicitly define intelligence in the technical sense.

Instead, understanding emerges through the progressive correction of perception and the restoration of awareness.

Knowledge is therefore associated less with prediction than with recognition of truth.

---

# Structural Relationships

The reconstructed structure can be summarized as follows.

```text
Reality
    │
    ▼
Perception
    │
    ▼
Representation
    │
 ┌──┴────────────┐
 ▼               ▼
Choice      Correction
 └──────┬────────┘
        ▼
New Perception
        │
        ▼
Representation Update
```

This structure represents the internal movement described throughout ACIM.

Rather than emphasizing external interaction, the primary transformation occurs through changes in perception and interpretation.

---

# Structural Characteristics

Several characteristics emerge from the reconstruction.

* Reality and perceived world are distinguished.
* Perception is considered transformable.
* Representation reflects the condition of the mind.
* Choice precedes correction.
* Correction continuously updates perception.
* Understanding develops through inner transformation.

These characteristics collectively define the reconstructed world representation of ACIM.

---

# Current Interpretation

From the perspective of structural reconstruction, ACIM presents a world representation centered on transformation of perception.

The primary dynamic is not the prediction of external events but the correction of internal interpretation.

The resulting world representation is therefore organized around recursive changes in perception rather than recursive interaction with the external environment.

This reconstruction serves as the ACIM-side reference for the comparative analysis developed in the subsequent chapters.
