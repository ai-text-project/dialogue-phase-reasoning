# Purpose

This document summarizes the concept of the **World Model** presented by Demis Hassabis during the referenced AI research lecture.

The purpose of this document is observation rather than comparison.

Interpretation and comparative analysis are intentionally deferred to later documents within this case.

---

# General Observation

Throughout the lecture, Demis Hassabis repeatedly emphasizes that modern AI systems should not be understood merely as pattern-matching systems.

Instead, they construct internal representations of the external world in order to support prediction, planning, and intelligent action.

The concept of a **World Model** appears as one of the central organizing ideas throughout the lecture.

---

# Observed Structural Components

The lecture repeatedly discusses several interconnected components.

These include:

- World
- Representation
- Search
- Prediction
- Planning
- Action
- Intelligence

Rather than appearing independently, these components are presented as an integrated system.

---

# Representation

The lecture suggests that intelligent systems construct internal representations of the external world.

These representations enable the system to organize information beyond simple memorization.

Representation therefore functions as an internal model supporting subsequent reasoning processes.

---

# Search

One of the most characteristic observations is the emphasis on **Search**.

The lecture repeatedly describes intelligence as the ability to efficiently search extremely large problem spaces.

Neural networks are presented not merely as classifiers, but as mechanisms that guide search toward promising regions within those spaces.

Search therefore appears as one of the central computational principles of the presented World Model.

---

# Prediction

Prediction is presented as a natural consequence of an internal world representation.

The better the internal representation,

the better the system can anticipate future states.

Prediction is therefore closely connected with planning and decision making.

---

# Planning

Planning extends prediction into sequential decision making.

The lecture describes planning as the process of selecting actions based upon internally simulated future possibilities.

Planning therefore depends upon both representation and prediction.

---

# Action

Action represents the external execution of internally generated plans.

The lecture treats action not as an isolated function but as the final stage of a larger cognitive process beginning with representation.

---

# Intelligence

Throughout the lecture, intelligence is consistently described as emerging from the interaction of multiple computational processes rather than from isolated knowledge.

The emphasis is placed upon:

- representation,
- search,
- prediction,
- planning,

and their coordination.

---

# Structural Observation

The lecture does not describe these concepts as independent modules.

Instead, they appear to form a continuous organizational process:

World

↓

Representation

↓

Search

↓

Prediction

↓

Planning

↓

Action

This organizational sequence is repeatedly implied throughout the lecture.

---
