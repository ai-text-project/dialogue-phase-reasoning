# Purpose

This document examines the organizing principles underlying two independent theoretical frameworks.

The first framework is the World Model presented by Demis Hassabis.

The second framework is the world representation described in *A Course in Miracles (ACIM)*.

The objective is not to compare definitions of intelligence.

Instead, this document investigates how each framework organizes the relationship among its internal components.

---

# AI Observation

Throughout the lecture, intelligence is not presented as an isolated capability.

Instead, intelligent behavior emerges through the coordinated interaction of several organizational components.

These include:

- Representation
- Search
- Prediction
- Planning
- Action

None of these components alone constitutes intelligence.

Rather,

intelligence appears to emerge from their coordinated organization.

Accordingly,

the World Model functions as an organizing framework rather than as a single computational module.

---

# ACIM Observation

Within ACIM,

experience likewise does not emerge from isolated concepts.

Instead,

multiple organizational processes interact.

These include:

- Mind
- Decision
- Projection
- Perception
- Experienced World

The mind serves as the origin of organization,

while projection and perception participate in the generation of experienced reality.

The framework therefore emphasizes the organization of experience rather than the accumulation of knowledge.

---

# Initial Structural Observation

Both frameworks describe systems composed of multiple interacting processes.

Neither framework attributes organization to a single isolated concept.

Instead,

organization emerges through structured relationships among multiple components.

Accordingly,

both systems appear to preserve an organizing principle that coordinates the entire system.

# Observed Difference

An important difference nevertheless remains.

Within the AI framework,

organization is directed toward adaptive interaction with the external world.

Within ACIM,

organization is directed toward the generation and interpretation of experience.

Thus,

the objectives of organization differ substantially,

even though both systems preserve internally coordinated structures.

---

# Research Note

At the current observational stage,

the comparison suggests that intelligence may be more appropriately understood as an organizational property than as an isolated function.

This observation remains provisional.

Whether organization itself constitutes the most appropriate comparative category requires further investigation.

No theoretical integration is proposed at this stage.

---

# Open Research Question

A new comparative question naturally emerges.

Rather than asking

"What is intelligence?"

the present observation suggests a different question.

"What organizes intelligence?"

This question remains intentionally open and will guide future comparative observations.
