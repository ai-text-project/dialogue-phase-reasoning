# Purpose

This document examines the relationship between **Action** and **Projection** within two independent theoretical frameworks.

The first framework is the World Model presented by Demis Hassabis.

The second framework is the world representation described in *A Course in Miracles (ACIM)*.

The objective is not to establish conceptual equivalence.

Instead, this document investigates whether both systems preserve comparable structural relationships between internal organization and externally experienced consequences.

---

# AI Observation

Within the World Model framework, action represents the external realization of internally organized processes.

Internal representation supports prediction.

Prediction supports planning.

Planning ultimately guides action.

The observed relationship may be summarized as:

Representation

↓

Prediction

↓

Planning

↓

Action

Action therefore represents the external consequence of an internally organized cognitive process.

---

# ACIM Observation

Within ACIM, projection functions as the process through which internal mental content becomes experienced as an external world.

The experienced world is therefore not treated as an independently existing origin.

Rather,

projection transforms internal organization into experienced perception.

The observed relationship may be summarized as:

Mind

↓

Projection

↓

Perception

↓

Experienced World

Projection therefore functions as the externalization of internal mental organization.

---

# Initial Structural Observation

Both frameworks distinguish between:

- an internally organized process,
- a transformational mechanism,
- and externally observable consequences.

Within the AI framework,

the transformation culminates in action.

Within ACIM,

the transformation culminates in projection and the experienced world.

Although the resulting phenomena differ,

both systems describe an organized transition from internal structure toward external manifestation.

---

---

# Observed Difference

A fundamental difference nevertheless remains.

Within the AI framework,

action is an adaptive response intended to influence the external environment.

Within ACIM,

projection does not primarily alter external reality.

Instead,

projection organizes how reality is experienced.

Thus,

AI emphasizes interaction with the environment,

whereas ACIM emphasizes the construction of experienced reality.

---

# Research Note

The comparison suggests that both frameworks require a mechanism connecting internal organization with externally observable consequences.

However,

the function of this mechanism differs.

AI describes behavioral execution.

ACIM describes experiential generation.

Whether these two organizational patterns preserve a deeper topological correspondence remains an open question.
