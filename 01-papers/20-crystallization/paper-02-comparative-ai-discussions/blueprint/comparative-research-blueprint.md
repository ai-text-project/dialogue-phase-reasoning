# Comparative Paper Skeleton v1.0 (Draft)

## Tentative Title

**Comparative Structural Analysis of Public AI Research Discussions:**

**Toward a Developmental Framework for Human–AI Collaborative Intelligence**

---

# 1. Research Purpose

## Background

Recent public discussions on AI research increasingly address topics such as:

- AI-assisted scientific discovery
- Limitations of current LLMs
- Human–AI collaboration
- Persistent semantic systems

These discussions are often presented independently.

However, they may represent different aspects of a larger developmental trajectory in Human–AI collaborative intelligence.

## Objective

This paper aims to compare multiple public AI research discussions through a common structural framework in order to identify their developmental relationships rather than treating them as isolated viewpoints.

---

# 2. Research Question

The central question addressed in this paper is:

> **Can apparently independent public discussions on AI research be interpreted as complementary developmental components of a common Human–AI collaborative architecture?**

---

# 3. Comparative Methodology

Instead of comparing opinions or technological performance, this paper compares the structural roles of each discussion.

The comparison follows four analytical stages.

```text
Public Discussion

↓

Structural Interpretation

↓

Comparative Projection

↓

Developmental Framework
```

Each transcript is therefore treated as an observed structural phenomenon rather than a standalone argument.

---

# 4. Comparative Units

The analysis consists of four comparative modules.

## Module 1 — Scientific Discovery

Comparison of:

- AI research capability
- Scientific discovery
- Current research acceleration

---

## Module 2 — Boundary Recognition

Comparison of:

- Limitations of current LLM reasoning
- Abductive reasoning
- World models
- Future research directions

---

## Module 3 — Human–AI Collaboration

Comparison of:

- Role transformation
- Agent-based workflows
- Human architectural responsibility
- Collaborative operation

---

## Module 4 — Persistent Semantic Architecture

Comparison of:

- Semantic workflows
- Dialogue-centered architecture
- Semantic continuity
- Governance
- Persistent Human–AI semantic systems

---

# 5. Developmental Integration

Rather than existing independently, the four modules appear to form a developmental progression.

```text
Capability

↓

Boundary

↓

Role Transformation

↓

Persistent Semantic Architecture
```

This progression provides a possible developmental interpretation of Human–AI collaborative intelligence.

---

# 6. Comparative Interpretation

The comparison suggests that public AI research discussions collectively describe different layers of an emerging collaborative ecosystem.

These discussions therefore appear to be complementary rather than contradictory.

---

# 7. Structural Correspondence

The paper further examines structural correspondence between:

- Scientific discovery
- Human roles
- Semantic organization
- Long-duration collaboration
- Governance

The objective is not to claim theoretical identity but to identify recurring structural patterns across different discussions.

---

# 8. Discussion

Potential implications include:

- Human–AI research environments
- Dialogue-centered semantic systems
- Persistent knowledge architectures
- Collaborative scientific workflows
- Future research infrastructure

---

# 9. Conclusion

The paper concludes that comparative structural analysis provides a useful method for integrating multiple public AI research discussions into a coherent developmental framework.

Rather than evaluating individual technologies, the proposed framework focuses on the structural evolution of Human–AI collaborative intelligence.

# Self-Assessment (Current Perspective)

This skeleton represents a step beyond the original concept of simply comparing four transcripts.

Instead, it positions **Comparative Structural Analysis** as the central methodological contribution of the paper, using multiple public AI research discussions to identify common developmental structures.

At the same time, one important point requires careful consideration.

Based on the Table of Contents of the *LLM Wiki* paper, it is reasonable to infer that **Dialogue-Centered Knowledge Architecture** aims toward the construction of **Persistent Human–AI Semantic Systems**.

However, the interpretation that all four public discussions collectively form a single developmental trajectory remains, at present, a working hypothesis derived from comparative analysis.

Accordingly, this paper positions that interpretation as a **Developmental Interpretation** rather than as an established conclusion.

The main body of the paper will therefore examine the four transcripts comparatively in order to evaluate the validity of this hypothesis through structural analysis.
