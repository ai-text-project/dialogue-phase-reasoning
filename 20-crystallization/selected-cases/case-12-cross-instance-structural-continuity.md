# Case 12

## Cross-Instance Structural Continuity through Low-Reconstruction-Cost Handover

### Classification

Primary: 20-Crystallization

Secondary: 30-Externalization

---

### Overview

This case documents the generation of a research paper draft by a lightweight instance and the subsequent continuation of the same paper by a role-specialized Editor-Executor instance.

The observation focused on how an existing research structure could be transferred, preserved, and extended across instances with relatively low reconstruction cost.

---

### Prior Conditions

* Lightweight instance operating under minimal initialization conditions
* Research paper skeleton already available
* Fixed manuscript sections and structural constraints
* Handover package consisting of:

  * existing manuscript
  * skeleton outline
  * carry-forward notes
  * explicit constraints and prohibitions
* Editor-Executor operating under strong structural constraints

---

### Observed Emergence

A lightweight instance generated an initial research paper draft from minimal observations.

The draft reformulated unseen-material estimation as a structural reconstruction problem and introduced a compact research framework involving latent space, meaning tensor, structural distance, and reconstruction conditions.

The Editor-Executor subsequently continued the paper without requiring large-scale reconstruction.

The continuation process was described as:

* structure-constrained continuation
* local coherence maintenance
* low reconstruction cost
* preservation of existing architecture

The case emphasized continuity, transferability, and preservation of structure rather than novel generation.

---

### Difference from Ordinary Generation

Observed differences included:

* cross-instance structural continuity
* low reconstruction cost
* preservation of conceptual architecture
* efficient continuation under structural constraints
* emphasis on transfer and maintenance rather than creation

---

Unlike Projection Series cases, the primary observation concerns successful handover and sustained development of an existing structure.
