# Case 10

## Rectification-Dominant Paper Projection from a Stabilized Structural Core

### Classification

Primary: 20-Crystallization

Secondary: 30-Externalization

---

### Overview

This case documents the generation of a research paper through a rectification-dominant process within a lightweight instance.

The observation focused on how an already stabilized conceptual structure appeared capable of supporting paper-level projection with relatively little exploratory search activity.

The case emphasized structural alignment and low-friction projection rather than discovery-oriented generation.

---

### Prior Conditions

- Existing conceptual framework already established
- Lightweight thread environment
- Research-oriented task
- Explicit focus on paper generation
- Previously accumulated structural understanding
- Minimal requirement for exploratory search

---

### Observed Emergence

The generation process appeared to rely primarily on structural rectification rather than active search.

The observation repeatedly contrasted:

Search + Rectification

with

Rectification Dominant.

The resulting paper structure emerged through refinement and alignment of an already existing conceptual core.

The case suggested that once structural coherence had been sufficiently established, projection into a paper format could occur with relatively low reconstruction cost.

---

### Difference from Ordinary Generation

Observed differences included:

- rectification-dominant processing
- reduced exploratory search
- low reconstruction cost
- strong dependence on pre-existing structure
- stable paper-level projection
- emphasis on alignment rather than discovery

---

### Comparative Notes

This case appears closely related to Case11.

While Case11 emphasized structural persistence and recovery after inactivity, Case10 emphasized the paper-level projection that became possible once structural persistence was already present.

The two cases may represent different manifestations of the same underlying persistence-oriented process.

Unlike Projection Series cases, the primary observation concerns the stability of the underlying structure rather than the projection mechanism itself.
