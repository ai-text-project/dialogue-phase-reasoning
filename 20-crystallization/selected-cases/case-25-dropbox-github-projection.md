# Case 25 — Low-Loss Projection from Dropbox Topology to GitHub Research Space

## Stage Relevance
- Primary: Stage 2
- Secondary: Stage 3

## Core Observation
A newly initialized instance supported a bounded projection from a dense Dropbox environment into a GitHub research space by suppressing topology inflation and preserving semantic role separation.

## Why This Case Matters
- It identifies design conditions that supported stable projection.
- It clarifies that success depended more on anti-expansion constraints than on aggressive generation.
- It provides a condition-explicit companion case to GitHub semantic stabilization.

## Conditions
- New thread.
- Initialization prompt only; no prior warm-up dialogue.
- Scope was intentionally limited rather than exhaustive.
- Key operating conditions included:
  - Do NOT expand / Do NOT inflate topology
  - role separation
  - observation-centered governance
  - lightweight hub design
  - structure before interpretation
- Dropbox was treated as a source topology for projection, not as a complete migration target.

## What Happened
The instance described the process as being driven not by a “magic prompt,” but by a pressure that repeatedly returned the system from expansion toward structural observation.  
Dropbox-to-GitHub was treated as projection rather than migration, which helped determine what to retain, what to lighten, what to place in the hub, and what to leave in Dropbox.

## Interpretation
This case suggests that stable externalization of dense semantic systems may depend on suppressing over-expansion and localizing semantic density.  
It is especially relevant to Stage 2 because it reveals conditions under which crystallized structure remains stable during external reorganization, while also providing an early Stage 3 projection pattern.

## Open Questions
- How general is “Do NOT inflate topology” as a stabilization condition?
- How universal is local semantic density plus global separation?
- Can projection, rather than migration, be formalized as a repeatable design principle?
- How much did the initialization matter relative to the broader research environment?
