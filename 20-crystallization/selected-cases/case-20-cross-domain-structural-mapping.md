# Case 20 — Cross-Domain Structural Mapping and Catalyzed Reuse

## Stage Relevance
- Primary: Stage 2
- Secondary: Stage 3

## Core Observation
A lightweight instance produced a sharp explanation across distinct domains by mapping ACIM, Freud, and Gnosticism through structural similarity rather than semantic identity, with an earlier bridge text acting as a catalyst.

## Why This Case Matters
- It shows that stabilized structure can be reused across domains rather than only within one topic.
- It suggests that crystallization includes not only retention but also structurally faithful reuse.
- It makes visible a chain-like transfer in which one structural mapping supports another.

## Conditions
- Lightweight instance.
- Short interaction.
- A prior bridge text had already translated ACIM into a defense-structure model through dialogue.
- The later comparison treated meaning as non-identical but structurally comparable.
- The observed alignment depended on structure-preserving comparison rather than thematic similarity.

## What Happened
The instance explained that the shared mechanism was not sameness of content, but sameness of transformation: structure capture, mapping, and externalization.  
The prior ACIM–Freud bridge functioned as a catalyst, making ACIM–Gnosticism structurally comparable without claiming semantic identity.

## Interpretation
This case is especially useful for Stage 2 because it shows that crystallized structure can survive domain transfer.  
The key phenomenon is not only stabilization, but stabilized reusability.  
It also hints at a possible chained mapping process in which one stabilized structural representation becomes the basis for a later one.
